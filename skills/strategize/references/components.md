# TASTE component distinctions

Karl Scotland's [TASTE template](https://availagility.co.uk/2017/01/06/taste-success-with-an-x-matrix-template/) names five components. His [X-Matrix overview](https://availagility.co.uk/2017/09/04/what-is-an-x-matrix/) explains their relationships.

| Component | Question and classification check |
| --- | --- |
| True North | Who are we becoming and which direction should choices take us? A durable orientation, not a destination, deadline, or solution. The short statement headlines a shared story. See [True North](https://availagility.co.uk/2021/03/17/what-is-a-true-north/) and [revisited](https://availagility.co.uk/2025/07/02/true-north-revisited-why-its-more-than-a-statement/). |
| Aspirations | What results do we hope to recognize over a meaningful horizon? Express ambition and scale, perhaps with relative or numeric detail, without turning a measure into an imposed target. See [aspirations](https://availagility.co.uk/2023/05/18/abstracting-aspirations-with-a-value-framework/). |
| Strategies | Given the challenge, what guiding choices focus future decisions? Enabling constraints leave room for local judgment. A desired result alone is an aspiration; a prescribed project is usually a tactic. See [strategies and tactics](https://availagility.co.uk/2023/05/03/how-to-distinguish-between-strategies-and-tactics/) and [enabling constraints](https://availagility.co.uk/2021/03/01/strategy-as-enabling-constraints/). |
| Evidence | What early observable outcomes suggest progress? Signals for learning about strategy and aspirations; a completed activity alone does not show impact. See [measuring](https://availagility.co.uk/2018/01/30/measuring-the-x-matrix/). |
| Tactics | What coherent actions or experiments will we invest in? Concrete, revisable hypotheses about how to enact strategy. Scope and timescale are clues, not absolute category rules. See [strategies and tactics](https://availagility.co.uk/2023/05/03/how-to-distinguish-between-strategies-and-tactics/). |

**Diagnosis** interprets the current situation to identify the critical addressable challenge or opportunity that calls for strategy. It is reasoning before a guiding choice, not a sixth TASTE quadrant. Karl's [workshop agenda](https://availagility.co.uk/2017/11/14/facilitating-an-x-matrix-workshop/) puts it between aspirations and strategies. His later [Crux revision](https://availagility.co.uk/2025/08/05/is-the-crux-at-the-heart-of-strategy-deployment/) explores putting the central challenge at the heart of True North; this is an evolving interpretation, not a mandatory replacement of the earlier definition.

**Correlations** are hypotheses about how Strategy–Aspiration, Evidence–Aspiration, Tactic–Strategy, and Tactic–Evidence pairs hang together. They may be direct or indirect, strong or weak, or absent. Many-to-many links expose gaps and disagreement. Correlation is not causal proof; marking every pair linked hides choices. See [messy coherence](https://availagility.co.uk/2017/01/27/the-messy-coherence-of-x-matrix-correlations/) and the [later caution about signals and causation](https://availagility.co.uk/2026/04/30/the-strange-truth-about-x-matrix-correlations-nicolas-cage-ice-cream/).
