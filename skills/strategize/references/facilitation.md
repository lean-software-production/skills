# Facilitate the conversation

Karl's [preferred order](https://availagility.co.uk/2024/02/15/introducing-the-order-of-the-x-matrix/) is: (1) True North, (2) Aspirations, (3) Strategies, (4) Strategy–Aspiration correlations, (5) Evidence, (6) Evidence–Aspiration correlations, (7) Tactics, (8) Tactic–Strategy correlations, (9) Tactic–Evidence correlations. It is iterative. Evidence precedes tactics so the group chooses signals of strategic progress before a favored activity biases measurement. Early correlation discussions expose mismatched assumptions. For component-only work, discuss relationships in words; no grid is required.

Begin with purpose and situational awareness. Ask what is happening, what the group hopes will change, and which addressable challenge or opportunity explains the gap. Park solution ideas while diagnosing. This adapts Karl's [workshop flow](https://availagility.co.uk/2017/11/14/facilitating-an-x-matrix-workshop/): explore context and options, then converge on choices and experiments.

When source material exists, synthesize a few candidate statements in your own words. Show what observations support each, what is inference, and whose perspective is missing. Ask: “What did I misread? What is missing? Which wording would change a real decision?” Revise with the group and record dissent. Drafts give people something concrete to correct; they are not decisions already made. Karl's [lessons learned](https://availagility.co.uk/2023/01/05/the-most-important-x-matrix-lessons-i-have-learned/) put collaboration ahead of a perfect document.

Test a strategy by asking whether it helps choose among plausible uses of attention or resources while leaving freedom in implementation. Optionally try **A even over B**: A and B must both be genuinely positive outcomes, and preferring A must carry a real cost to B. This frames outcomes, not an activity against a straw man. If that choice is not real, remove the format and revisit the diagnosis. A forced tradeoff is counterproductive. See [deploying strategies as choices](https://availagility.co.uk/2019/02/08/deploying-strategies-as-choices/).

At each review, keep wording, status (agreed, candidate, unresolved), reason, and next question visible. Ask what evidence would change minds. Revisit components as context changes. Introduce a drawn matrix only when the group wants to examine the full pattern; [Karl notes](https://availagility.co.uk/2023/01/05/the-most-important-x-matrix-lessons-i-have-learned/) that forcing the format early reduces its value.
